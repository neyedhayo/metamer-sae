Metamer-SAE, pilot review

What the pilot shows, and what we should do next

Samuel's pilot asks which internal visual features differ between metamers from a standard and an adversarially trained ResNet-50. This page reruns his pipeline from scratch on Engaging, shows the outputs, and lists the questions they raise.

Rerun on MIT Engaging, 25 Nov 2026, job 30480649, repo commit 95e8aed. Stimuli from Feather et al. (2023). The extra descriptives marked "new" are quick, exploratory numbers on the 32 primary triplets, not confirmatory tests.

The setup

One source image, two metamers, one outside observer

Each metamer matches its source at layer 2 of a ResNet-50, either standard or adversarially trained. A separate frozen network (ConvNeXt-Tiny, stage 3, 384 pooled channels) then measures how far each metamer moved from its source. Numbers below are that distance (raw L2).

Reading

To this observer a standard layer-2 metamer is almost as far from its source as a different photo of the same class, even though a person sees the same picture. An adversarial metamer sits about as close as a natural near neighbour.

Natural distances are medians over Imagenette validation activations (3,925 images, 10 classes).

So far the picture looks less like "standard metamers lose particular features" and more like "all metamers pick up a common signature, standard ones more of it".

Output 4, new

Three reasons to be careful with the headline

Pixels

Standard metamers are also farther in pixels

0.150 vs 0.070

RMS pixel difference from the source, standard larger in 32 of 32. The simplest baseline already predicts the direction of the result.

PCA

Sixteen components carry the effect

1.58 of 1.63

The L2 gap in PCA-16 is nearly the whole raw gap. The SAE is not needed to see it.

Stability

SAE features do not repeat across seeds

0.26

mean cosine between best-matched decoder atoms of the two seeds. Only 10% of latents have a match above 0.5. Feature identities are not yet interpretable.

The pilot write-up already says the SAE is not shown to beat PCA. These numbers say the same thing more sharply. Per source, pixel distance only weakly tracks observer distance (Spearman 0.10 standard, 0.30 adversarial), so pixels do not explain everything.

Research questions

What the outputs make us want to know

  1. What is the shared direction? Most of the shift is common to every source. Is it a texture or high-frequency signature of the optimisation, a contrast change, something else? Does source identity survive once it is removed?
  2. Is adversarial training a different kind of change, or the same change at half strength? Direction and SAE profile agree at 0.99. Where, if anywhere, do the two conditions differ in kind?
  3. Does the observer result go beyond pixel distance? Compare with noise or blur matched in pixel RMS, and with LPIPS and SSIM, before reading it as a feature finding.
  4. Does any of this track what people see? Layer 2 is where both metamers stay recognisable. The human divergence in Feather et al. is at later layers. Do these measures follow human recognition across layers?
  5. What would make the SAE earn its place over PCA? Features that repeat across seeds, that can be named, and that separate conditions in a way 16 principal components cannot.
  6. Does the answer depend on the observer? ConvNeXt-Tiny is itself standard-trained. Would a robust observer, or the generator's own later layers, tell the same story?
Proposal

What we should do next

1, this week, no new data

Controls on the 48 triplets we have

Pixel, SSIM, LPIPS baselines (the command exists: image-baselines). Pixel-matched noise control through the same observer. Report distances in natural-image units. Characterise the shared direction, then redo source retrieval with it removed. Do exploratory work on the 8 development triplets, since the new numbers here already used the primary 32.

2, next, same pipeline

All layers and all 397 triplets for ResNet-50

Layer 2 is the least informative stage. Sweep the generation layers, then line each condition up with Feather's published human recognition, at the condition level. This is the step that connects to perception.

3, in parallel

Decide whether the SAE stays

Train on a broader natural set than Imagenette (10 classes, 9.5k images for 1,536 latents), try spatial activations instead of pooled, and set a cross-seed stability bar before interpreting any latent. If features still do not repeat, report PCA as the main analysis.

4, after 1 to 3

AlexNet and IPCL

Only 3 of 9 registry conditions resolve against Feather's metadata today (ResNet-50 standard and robust, AlexNet standard). The rest need manual pinning, and matrix-inventory crashes on unresolved entries, so the audit cannot finish yet. Adversarial IPCL does not exist in Feather's release.

Notes

What ran, and what had to be fixed to run it

  • Ran the pilot end to end from a fresh clone: 144 Feather images, 13,394 Imagenette images, ConvNeXt extraction, new PCA, new TopK SAEs (seeds 123 and 42), paired evaluation with 10,000 bootstrap resamples. About five minutes on one H100. 41 tests pass, 1 skipped (needs the archived pilot artifacts).

  • Three setup problems for Samuel: overcomplete 0.3.0 needs matplotlib < 3.9; importing Feather's metadata pulls in h5py, dill, torchaudio and chcochleagram, which are not in pyproject.toml; matrix-inventory raises KeyError: nan for unresolved registry conditions.
  • Everything is in /orcd/data/jhm/001/dlatorre/metamer-sae on Engaging, with a local copy in metamer-sae/engaging_results/. The follow-up numbers come from outputs/followup/followup.py.
  • One number to treat as soft: the natural-pair baseline for SAE feature overlap came out at 0.19 for one seed and 0.08 for the other, so it is left out above until checked.
